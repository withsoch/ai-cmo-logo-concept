Munshi, the scribe who listens.

A munshi was the person who listened and wrote on someone's behalf: letters, records, speeches. That is the product: it hears your week and writes in your voice. Four directions below, each shown large, on dark, as an app icon, at favicon size, and in a header.

Speech nib

Recommended

Direction D · mascot

Munshi the ink drop

A friendly ink-drop character with a saffron spark: the mascot for onboarding, empty states, the interview screen and social posts. Deliberately abstract, so the name carries the heritage and the character never turns into a caricature. Pairs best with Direction A as the logo.

Stickers and avatars

My recommendation

Direction A as the logo, Direction D as the mascot. The speech nib is the only mark that explains the product without words (hears, then writes), which matters most for the integrations story you are pitching. The ink drop gives the brand warmth in the places a logo can't: the interview, empty states, social.

Before committing to the name: check the trademark in your target markets (EU, UK, US) and the domains (munshi.ai, getmunshi.com, munshi.app). "Munshi" is a common word and surname in South Asia, so expect existing businesses using it; what matters is whether any are in software or marketing.

These are concept sketches drawn in code, not final artwork. Once you pick a direction, a designer should refine the curves and spacing before it goes on anything printed.

Round 2 recommendation

A system, not a single pick: E (munshi is typing…) as the everyday logo and voice of the brand, G (truck art) as the launch and campaign layer, and H (voice into ink) as the motion signature on Reels and video. F, the ledger, is the best merch object if you want one physical thing at events.

E beats Round 1's A for marketing because it becomes a phrase people say ("let munshi handle it"), and G is the part only you can credibly own: a Pakistani founder in Tallinn bringing truck art to B2B LinkedIn is a story, not just a style.

Round 3 recommendation

I, the talking nib, is the merge that works. It keeps Direction A's shape, so it reads at 16 pixels, and adds H's motion: sound pulses inside the bubble and ink writes itself out of the nib. As a still it is a confident logo; as an animation it explains the product in under four seconds. That is the one to take to a designer.

Keep two supporting pieces: Q (greatest hits) for campaigns and merch, because "your best lines, on repeat" is a whole content series, and N (wink) as the mascot face in the app and on social. K, the one-line bubble, is the most elegant, but its thin stroke disappears at small sizes.
